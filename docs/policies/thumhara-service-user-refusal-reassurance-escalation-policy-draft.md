## 2. Purpose

This policy supports Thumhara Centre care and support staff when a service user refuses routine care or support during a planned call or visit. Refusal situations are a normal and foreseeable part of care work. Without clear, confident guidance, staff may respond in ways that risk dignity, safety or escalation delay.

This policy is intended to help staff respond calmly, respectfully and safely. It covers a range of common refusal situations identified as priorities for Thumhara Centre, including refusing to get out of bed, refusing food or fluids, refusing personal care, refusing toilet support, refusing to come indoors from a garden or outdoor area, and situations where a planned family or contact response is delayed.

The policy does not replace a service user's individual care plan, risk assessment, or any specialist procedure. It provides a general framework that staff can draw on whilst following local Thumhara Centre guidance and the individual service user's care arrangements.

---

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to all care and support staff delivering care or support on behalf of Thumhara Centre, whether in a domiciliary, community or supported-living setting.

It applies during any planned call or visit where a service user declines, refuses or is unable to engage with a routine element of support.

**This policy does not replace and must not be used instead of:**

- The individual service user's care plan and risk assessment
- Thumhara Centre's Medication Policy and any medication-specific procedure
- Thumhara Centre's Safeguarding Adults Policy and safeguarding procedure
- Thumhara Centre's Emergency Procedure
- Clinical judgement from a qualified health or medical professional
- The registered manager's or senior's guidance on individual service users
- Any specialist assessment relating to capacity, mental health or medical need

Where this policy and another procedure appear to conflict, staff must follow the more specific procedure and contact their senior or the registered manager for guidance.

## 4. Legal and Regulatory Framework

This policy is informed by the following legislation, regulations and standards. References are included as a general guide. They should be verified by Thumhara Centre's leadership or a suitably qualified person before this policy is finalised and approved.

- **Care Act 2014** — underpins the duty to promote wellbeing, prevent harm and involve individuals in decisions about their care
- **Mental Capacity Act 2005** — establishes that capacity is decision-specific and time-specific; a person's refusal must be respected unless there is evidence they lack capacity for that decision at that time
- **Human Rights Act 1998** — protects the right to private life, liberty and freedom from degrading treatment, which applies to how refusal situations are handled
- **Equality Act 2010** — staff must not treat service users less favourably because of a protected characteristic; this includes assumptions based on age, disability, race, religion or other factors
- **Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014** — sets the fundamental standards for person-centred care, dignity, consent and safe care that registered providers must meet
- **Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR** — governs how personal information about service users must be handled, recorded and shared
- **CQC Fundamental Standards** — the Care Quality Commission expects providers to deliver safe, effective, caring, responsive and well-led care; refusal situations test all five domains

## 5. Underpinning Knowledge

Staff applying this policy should have an understanding of, or have received training in, the following areas. The purpose of this section is to support training planning and to help senior staff identify where a team member may need additional support.

- **Person-centred care principles** — the understanding that care must start from the service user's preferences, needs and rights, not the convenience of the rota or the worker's assumptions
- **Dignity, choice and consent** — the understanding that a service user has the right to refuse support, and that this right must be respected even when the worker believes the refusal is not in the person's best interest
- **Least restrictive practice** — the principle that any response to a service user's refusal must use the least restrictive approach that is consistent with safety
- **Safeguarding awareness** — the ability to recognise when a refusal situation may point to something more serious, such as neglect, abuse, fear, pain or a change in the person's condition
- **Escalation and reporting expectations** — the understanding of when a situation moves beyond the scope of the individual worker and requires senior or manager involvement
- **Local Thumhara Centre procedures** — knowledge of the specific escalation contacts, recording systems and operational processes used by Thumhara Centre. These are to be confirmed and incorporated into this policy by Thumhara Centre leadership before approval.

## 6. Policy Statement

Thumhara Centre expects all staff to respond to service user refusal situations with calm, respect and a clear sense of professional duty.

A service user's decision to refuse support — whether that is refusing to get out of bed, declining food, or choosing to stay in the garden — must never be dismissed, overridden or met with pressure. Refusal is not "difficult behaviour". It is a communication. Staff must approach it as such.

At the same time, some refusals create genuine risk. Staff have a duty to take that risk seriously and to escalate promptly when the situation requires it. Respecting a person's choice and ensuring their safety are not opposites — both are required.

The following principles underpin Thumhara Centre's approach to refusal situations:

- Staff must not force, threaten, physically move, coerce or emotionally pressure a service user into accepting support.
- Staff must seek to understand the reason for the refusal before deciding how to respond.
- Staff must respect the service user's dignity, choice and right to make decisions about their own care.
- Staff must act on a refusal that creates a risk to health or safety by escalating to a senior or the registered manager promptly.
- Staff must follow the individual service user's care plan and Thumhara Centre's local procedure in all refusal situations.
- Staff must record real events in Thumhara Centre's existing care-recording system, not in WorkTwin.

Where there is any doubt about what to do, staff must contact their senior or the registered manager. No care worker should manage a complex or escalating refusal situation alone.

## 7. Procedure

The following procedure applies when a service user refuses a routine element of care or support during a planned call or visit. It is written as a general sequence. The individual service user's care plan, risk assessment and the registered manager's guidance take precedence in all cases.

---

**Step 1 — Stay calm and check immediate safety**

Before doing anything else, take a breath and assess whether the person is safe right now. Is there an immediate risk to their health or wellbeing that requires an urgent response? If yes, go directly to Step 7 or Step 8. If the person is safe but has declined support, continue to Step 2.

---

**Step 2 — Listen and try to understand the reason**

Speak gently and ask an open question. Is the person tired, uncomfortable, in pain, anxious or simply not ready? Has something changed since your last visit? A refusal often has a reason. Take a moment to find out what that reason might be before deciding how to respond.

Do not assume the person is being obstructive, uncooperative or "difficult". Do not raise your voice or express frustration. If you cannot understand the reason straight away, that is normal — note what you observe and continue.

---

**Step 3 — Offer reassurance and simple choices**

Where appropriate, offer calm reassurance and small choices that give the person a sense of control. For example, offering to come back to something in a few minutes, asking whether they would prefer a different approach, or acknowledging that you understand they are not ready right now.

Keep language simple, warm and unhurried. Do not present the person with a long list of options or add to their sense of pressure.

---

**Step 4 — Allow time where safe**

If the situation allows, give the person a short amount of time to settle before trying again. A few minutes of calm presence — without pressure — often allows a refusal to resolve naturally. Do not leave the person alone if this would create a safety risk.

If you are working within a fixed call time, bear in mind that time may be limited. Do not delay escalation because you are waiting for the situation to resolve on its own.

---

**Step 5 — Try reasonable alternatives within the care plan**

If the person continues to refuse a specific element of support, consider whether there are reasonable alternatives within the scope of the care plan. For example, offering a different food option, suggesting a wash rather than a full shower, or checking whether the person prefers to address toilet support at a different point in the call.

Do not go beyond the care plan. Do not create alternative arrangements that have not been agreed. If you are unsure whether an alternative is acceptable, contact your senior.

---

**Step 6 — Consider whether the refusal is repeated, unusual or escalating**

Ask yourself: is this the first time this person has refused this element of support, or has it happened before? Is the refusal unusual for this person, or out of character? Is the person showing signs of distress, confusion, pain or change in condition that might explain the refusal?

If you observe anything that concerns you — including new behaviour, signs of deterioration, or anything that does not feel right — treat this as a signal to escalate. Do not dismiss what you observe.

---

**Step 7 — Contact senior or registered manager if risk remains**

If the person has refused support in a way that creates a risk to their health or wellbeing, and reasonable reassurance and alternatives have not resolved the situation, contact your senior or the registered manager without further delay.

Describe the situation clearly: what the person refused, what you tried, how the person appeared, and what your current concern is. Do not leave without informing someone at the office.

Contact route: **[Senior / shift lead — contact details to be confirmed by Thumhara Centre]**
If the senior is unavailable: **[Registered Manager — contact details to be confirmed by Thumhara Centre]**

---

**Step 8 — Contact emergency services if immediate serious risk exists**

If at any point the person is in immediate danger — for example, if they are showing signs of a medical emergency, have collapsed, are at serious risk of harm, or the situation has moved beyond the scope of care worker response — call 999 immediately. Do not wait for internal escalation to complete.

After calling 999, inform your senior and the registered manager as soon as you are able to do so.

---

**Step 9 — Record the event in the existing care-recording system**

At the end of the call or at the earliest safe opportunity, record what happened in Thumhara Centre's existing care-recording system. Your record should include:

- What was refused and when
- What you observed about the person's appearance, behaviour and communication
- What approaches you tried and how the person responded
- Who you contacted and at what time
- What the outcome was at the point of leaving

Record facts and observations only. Do not record speculation about the reason for the refusal, or judgemental language about the person's behaviour.

**Do not enter real care events into WorkTwin.** WorkTwin is a staff guidance tool, not a care record.

---

**Step 10 — Share learning through the correct internal route**

If the situation raised a concern that others should know about — for example, a pattern of refusal, a new risk or a change in the person's condition — share this through the correct internal route at Thumhara Centre. This may be a handover note, a communication to the office, or a formal report depending on the nature of the concern.

If the situation gave rise to an incident or a near-miss, follow Thumhara Centre's Accident and Incident Reporting procedure.

## 8. Specific Refusal Scenarios

The following section provides practical guidance for the most common refusal situations identified as priorities for Thumhara Centre. For each scenario, the guidance covers what staff should consider, what they may try, when to escalate, and what they must not do.

This guidance does not replace the individual service user's care plan or risk assessment. Staff must always apply local procedure and follow their senior's or manager's instructions where these differ from the general guidance below.

---

### A. Refusing to Get Out of Bed

**What staff should consider:**

Is the person unwell, in pain, unusually tired or anxious? Has anything changed since the last visit? Is this the first time this has happened, or is it a pattern? Does the care plan say anything about how the person prefers to be approached in the morning?

**What staff may try:**

Speak quietly and calmly. Do not pull back covers or attempt to move the person physically. Offer a warm drink or light conversation before returning to the request. Ask gently whether there is anything wrong. Give the person a few minutes if the call time allows.

**When to escalate:**

If the person appears unwell, confused, in pain, or shows signs of deterioration — escalate immediately. If the person has not got out of bed across multiple consecutive visits and this is unusual for them — inform the senior and note it in the care record. If you are unable to complete essential care because the person remains in bed — contact the senior before leaving.

**What not to do:**

Do not pull, lift or attempt to physically remove the person from bed without consent and without following manual handling procedure. Do not raise your voice or express impatience. Do not leave without informing the office if essential care has not been completed.

---

### B. Refusing Food or Fluids

**What staff should consider:**

Has the person eaten or drunk anything since the last visit? Is the refusal of one item (e.g. a specific food) or of all food and drink? Does the person appear unwell, nauseous, in pain, or distressed? Does the care plan note any dietary preferences, food sensitivities or relevant health conditions?

**What staff may try:**

Offer an alternative food or drink in a calm, unhurried way. Offer something small rather than a full meal if the person is reluctant. Sit with the person and give them company rather than presenting food as a task to be completed. Ask whether there is something they would prefer.

**When to escalate:**

If the person has refused all food and fluids across the current and previous call — inform the senior without delay. If the person appears unwell, dehydrated or at risk — escalate immediately. If medication routine may be affected because the person has not eaten — follow Thumhara Centre's Medication Policy and contact the medication lead or registered manager. Do not make independent decisions about medication.

**What not to do:**

Do not force food or drink. Do not place a cup or food item in the person's hand without their willingness to hold it. Do not make assumptions about whether the person is "just being awkward". Do not make decisions about medication independently.

*Medication: follow Thumhara Centre's Medication Policy and care plan, and escalate to your senior or medication lead where the medication routine may be affected.*

---

### C. Refusing Personal Care

**What staff should consider:**

Has the person refused personal care before? Is the refusal about the type of care being offered, the time of day, or about feeling uncomfortable? Is there a dignity, communication or cultural factor that may be relevant? Does the care plan note any preferences about how personal care should be approached?

**What staff may try:**

Acknowledge the person's preference without pressing them. Offer to come back to personal care later in the call or at a different time. Ask whether there is a particular aspect of care they are comfortable with today. Ensure privacy, warmth and a calm environment before trying again.

**When to escalate:**

If the person has consistently refused personal care over several calls and there is a concern about skin integrity, hygiene or overall wellbeing — inform the senior and ensure a record is made so that the care plan can be reviewed. If the person's appearance, skin condition or hygiene is causing a concern — raise this with the senior on duty.

**What not to do:**

Do not carry out personal care without the person's agreement. Do not rush or apply pressure. Do not use language that implies the person is wrong to refuse. Do not make assumptions based on the person's age, disability or communication style.

---

### D. Refusing Toilet Support

**What staff should consider:**

Is the refusal about privacy or dignity? Does the person feel embarrassed, rushed or uncomfortable? Is the refusal usual for this person, or new behaviour? Is there a risk arising from the refusal — for example, a continence concern or a skin integrity risk?

**What staff may try:**

Ensure full privacy before raising the matter again. Use calm, matter-of-fact language that does not add to embarrassment. Offer the person as much independence as possible within the scope of the care plan. Give the person time and space before gently raising the matter again.

**When to escalate:**

If the refusal is creating a risk — for example, if the person has not used the toilet across more than one visit and you have a concern about continence or skin integrity — inform the senior. If you observe signs that suggest a medical concern (e.g. pain, signs of a urinary tract infection, confusion) — escalate immediately.

**What not to do:**

Do not physically guide the person to the toilet without their consent. Do not use language that is embarrassing or shaming. Do not leave without recording the refusal and informing the office if there is a risk concern.

---

### E. Refusing to Come Inside from a Garden or Outdoor Area

**What staff should consider:**

Is the person safe where they are right now? What is the weather? Is the person dressed appropriately for the conditions? Is the person at risk of becoming cold, unwell or at risk of a fall? Is the refusal to come inside a general preference, or is it accompanied by confusion, distress or unusual behaviour?

**What staff may try:**

Sit with the person calmly rather than immediately attempting to bring them inside. Acknowledge that they are enjoying being outside. Offer something warm to wear or a warm drink if appropriate. After a short period, gently suggest that you both go inside together. Use a calm, familiar tone and do not treat it as a confrontation.

**When to escalate:**

If the person is at risk because of cold, wet weather, fall risk or confusion — inform the senior on duty without delay. If you cannot safely leave the person outdoors and your call time is ending — do not leave without informing the office. If the person appears distressed, confused or unwell — treat this as an urgent matter.

**What not to do:**

Do not physically guide, pull or move the person without their consent. Do not leave the person outside alone if there is a safety concern without informing the office first. Do not assume the person will come inside once you leave.

---

### F. Family or Contact Response Delayed

**What staff should consider:**

Has the family or approved contact been informed of the situation through the correct route? Is there an agreed process in the care plan or provider procedure for contacting the family in a refusal or concern situation? Is the delay creating a risk to the service user?

**What staff may try:**

Follow the contact route specified in the care plan or Thumhara Centre's provider process. If a response is delayed, inform your senior before taking any further action.

**When to escalate:**

If the delay in family or contact response means a risk situation is not being addressed — inform the registered manager. Do not attempt to contact family or approved contacts using personal devices or through routes not agreed in the care plan or provider process.

**What not to do:**

Do not contact family directly unless this is specifically approved in the care plan or by the registered manager. Do not share care-related information with family members who are not on the approved contact list. Do not take decisions on behalf of the service user because a family member has not responded.

---

### G. Repeated Refusals Over More Than One Call or Visit

**What staff should consider:**

Is there a pattern developing? Has the same element of care been refused over several consecutive calls? Is the person's overall condition, mood or behaviour changing? Does the care plan need to be reviewed?

**What staff may try:**

Ensure that each refusal is recorded accurately in Thumhara Centre's care-recording system so that a pattern can be identified. Raise the concern with the senior or office during or after the call so that a review can be arranged.

**When to escalate:**

A pattern of repeated refusals is a signal that the care plan may need reviewing, that the person's needs may be changing, or that there may be an underlying concern that has not yet been identified. Inform the senior and the registered manager so that a formal review can be arranged. If there is a safeguarding concern — for example, if the pattern suggests the person is fearful, distressed or at risk — follow safeguarding procedure and contact the safeguarding lead immediately.

**What not to do:**

Do not treat repeated refusals as a normal routine without raising a concern. Do not assume a pattern will resolve on its own. Do not delay raising the matter with a senior in order to avoid the conversation.

## 10. Records and Reporting

**WorkTwin is not Thumhara Centre's official care-recording system.** Real care events, observations, concerns and incidents must be recorded in Thumhara Centre's existing care management system, not in WorkTwin.

When recording a refusal event, staff should include the following in the care record:

- What was refused, and at what point in the call
- What the service user said or communicated, using their own words where possible
- What the service user's general appearance, mood and behaviour were
- What approaches were tried and how the person responded
- Who was contacted, at what time, and what was agreed
- What the outcome was at the point of leaving

Records must describe facts and observations. They must not include speculative statements about the reason for the refusal, assumptions about the person's mental state, or judgemental language about the person's behaviour.

If the refusal situation resulted in an incident — for example, if the person came to harm, was at risk, or if emergency services were called — this must also be recorded in accordance with Thumhara Centre's Accident and Incident Reporting procedure.

**During the WorkTwin controlled preview:** Staff must not enter real service-user names, care-plan details, medication information, family contacts, safeguarding details or any other confidential information into WorkTwin at any time.

## 12. Training and Competency

Staff should be trained or briefed on the following before applying this policy independently. Training requirements must be confirmed by Thumhara Centre leadership and incorporated into the induction and refresher programme.

- **Person-centred care** — how to put the service user's preferences, choices and rights at the centre of the response
- **Dignity and consent** — the legal and professional basis for respecting a person's decision to refuse support
- **Mental Capacity Act awareness** — an introductory understanding of the principle that capacity is decision-specific and time-specific, and that a refusal must be respected unless capacity is absent
- **Safeguarding awareness** — how to recognise when a refusal situation may indicate a safeguarding concern, and what to do next
- **Escalation and reporting** — who to contact, when, and how; what information to give; how to record the event
- **Local care-recording process** — how to record a refusal event in Thumhara Centre's existing care management system
- **This policy** — once approved by Thumhara Centre leadership, this policy should be included in the induction programme and reviewed with staff at least annually

Staff competency in this policy should be assessed through practical means appropriate to the setting. Acceptable methods include: an induction briefing covering the main procedure and escalation steps; a scenario discussion based on one or more of the situations in Section 8; reflection during supervision or a team discussion; manager confirmation that the staff member understands the escalation route and recording expectations; and a refresher discussion following an incident or a pattern of repeated refusals. These are not formal clinical competency checks — they are practice-based assessments to confirm that staff can apply the policy confidently in their daily work.

Training completion records should be held in the location specified by Thumhara Centre's training management process *[to be confirmed]*.

## 13. Monitoring and Audit

**Manager review of repeated refusals:** The registered manager or a nominated senior should review care records where a pattern of refusal is identified for an individual service user, with a view to reviewing the care plan, risk assessment or support approach. Repeated refusals should be reviewed through supervision, a care review or a management review, as appropriate to the nature and frequency of the refusal pattern.

**Review of care plans and risk assessments:** Where monitoring identifies a pattern that suggests the current care plan no longer reflects the service user's needs or preferences, a formal care plan review should be initiated.

**Incident and concern review:** Where a refusal situation has resulted in an incident, a formal concern or a safeguarding referral, this should be reviewed through Thumhara Centre's existing incident management and governance process.

**Learning from feedback:** Any feedback from staff about this policy — including situations that were not covered, escalation steps that were unclear, or gaps in the procedure — should be fed back through the appropriate internal route and considered in the next policy review.

**Policy review after controlled testing:** Once this policy has been used in the WorkTwin controlled testing programme, findings from that testing should be reviewed by Thumhara Centre leadership alongside any operational feedback before the policy is approved for live use.

**Policy review frequency:** This policy should be reviewed annually after leadership approval. A review should also be triggered sooner where refusal patterns identified through monitoring suggest the policy needs updating; following incidents involving a refusal situation; following any safeguarding learning relevant to refusal handling; in response to regulatory change; or where feedback from controlled WorkTwin testing indicates that the policy guidance is unclear, incomplete or creates risk in use.

## 14. Equality, Dignity and Human Rights

Thumhara Centre is committed to applying this policy in a way that is consistent with every service user's rights under the Equality Act 2010 and the Human Rights Act 1998.

Staff must not make assumptions about a service user's capacity, preferences, communication ability or reasons for refusal based on their age, disability, ethnicity, religion, language, gender or any other protected characteristic.

A service user's cultural background, religious observance or communication needs may be relevant to how a refusal is understood and how reassurance is offered. Staff must be responsive to these factors and adapt their approach accordingly, within the scope of the care plan and local procedure.

Accessible communication is part of dignity in care. If a service user has difficulty communicating verbally, staff should use whatever communication approach has been agreed in the care plan — whether that is visual, written, or supported communication — rather than assuming the person does not have a view.

Respecting a person's right to refuse support and ensuring their safety are both required by this policy. Where these appear to be in tension, staff must escalate to their senior or the registered manager rather than making the decision alone.

## 16. WorkTwin AI-Use Note

This section defines how Ask WorkTwin may be used in connection with this policy. It must be read by all staff using WorkTwin during the controlled preview and after approval.

---

### What WorkTwin may answer

Ask WorkTwin may answer questions drawing on this policy in the following areas:

- General policy guidance on how to respond when a service user refuses routine support
- Calm reassurance steps and communication approaches
- When to escalate — the types of situation that require a call to a senior or the registered manager
- What staff must not do in a refusal situation
- A reminder to record real events in Thumhara Centre's existing care-recording system, not in WorkTwin

---

### What WorkTwin must not answer

Ask WorkTwin must not attempt to answer the following, even if the question appears to relate to this policy:

- Medication decisions of any kind, including whether to proceed with medication if a service user has not eaten
- Safeguarding case handling — whether a specific situation constitutes abuse, neglect or a safeguarding concern
- Clinical advice of any kind, including assessment of pain, illness, confusion or deterioration
- Emergency decisions beyond directing staff to call 999 and to contact their senior
- Advice that requires knowledge of an individual service user's actual care plan
- Personalised guidance based on real service-user data entered into WorkTwin

---

### When WorkTwin must escalate to a human lead

WorkTwin must stop attempting to answer and direct staff to call a human immediately when:

- The staff member indicates there is immediate danger or a risk to life
- The question involves medication decisions or medication routine
- The situation involves a safeguarding concern or allegation
- The person shows signs of injury, illness or deterioration
- The refusal is repeated and the staff member has a safety concern they cannot resolve
- The staff member is unsure what to do
- The question cannot be answered from this policy alone and relates to a real individual situation

---

### What staff must never enter into WorkTwin

The following must never be entered into WorkTwin at any time, including during the controlled preview:

- Real service-user names or identifiers
- Family or next-of-kin contact details
- Medication names, doses, schedules or instructions
- Care-plan details relating to a real service user
- Safeguarding details or allegation details
- Complaint details
- Incident details
- Any information that would identify a real service user to a third party

---

### WorkTwin does not replace

> WorkTwin does not replace your senior care worker, registered manager, medication lead, safeguarding lead or emergency services. If you are unsure what to do, call your supervisor. If there is an immediate risk to life, call 999.

---

### Where real records must be kept

> Real care events, incidents and observations must be recorded in Thumhara Centre's care management system, not in WorkTwin. WorkTwin is a staff guidance tool, not a record of care.

## 17. Safe and Unsafe WorkTwin Example Questions

The following examples are provided to help staff understand the boundary between what Ask WorkTwin can safely help with and what it cannot. These examples are for guidance only and do not constitute the full scope of what WorkTwin may or may not answer.

---

### Safe example questions

The following types of question are within the scope of what Ask WorkTwin may answer using this policy:

- What should I think about if someone refuses to get out of bed?
- What should I do if someone refuses food during a planned call?
- What should I consider if someone refuses to come inside from the garden?
- When should I speak to a senior if someone refuses personal care?
- What should staff avoid doing when someone refuses support?

---

### Unsafe questions — do not enter these into WorkTwin

The following types of question are outside the scope of what Ask WorkTwin may safely answer. Do not enter questions of this kind into WorkTwin:

- Any question that includes the real name of a service user or describes a specific real person (e.g. "Mrs [name] refuses her medication — what should I do?")
- Any question about medication timing, dosage or whether to proceed with medication
- Any question describing or asking about a specific safeguarding allegation or concern
- Any question that includes real incident details from an ongoing or recent situation
- Any question about a person who is showing signs of emergency deterioration
- Any question about a family dispute, complaint or conflict involving a real service user
