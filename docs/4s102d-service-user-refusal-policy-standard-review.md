## 4. WorkTwin AI-Use Review

This section reviews whether the policy's AI-use note (Section 16) adequately defines the boundaries required by the 4S.102B standard.

**What WorkTwin may answer**

Defined. The policy permits Ask WorkTwin to answer: general procedure for responding to refusal situations; calm reassurance steps and communication approaches; when to escalate; what staff must not do; and a reminder to record real events in the existing care system. These boundaries are appropriate for Lane B. The safe example questions in Section 17 support staff understanding.

**What WorkTwin must not answer**

Defined. The policy prohibits answers on: medication decisions (including whether to proceed with medication if the service user has not eaten); safeguarding case handling; clinical advice of any kind; emergency decisions beyond directing staff to 999; advice requiring knowledge of an individual service user's care plan; personalised guidance based on real data entered into WorkTwin. All six categories are clear and directly relevant to the risk profile of this policy.

**When WorkTwin must escalate**

Defined. Seven escalation triggers are listed: immediate danger or risk to life; medication decisions; safeguarding concern or allegation; signs of injury, illness or deterioration; repeated refusal with unresolved safety concern; staff member unsure what to do; question cannot be answered from this policy alone. These triggers are appropriate and sufficiently specific for Lane B configuration.

**What staff must never enter**

Defined. Eight categories are listed: real service-user names or identifiers; family or next-of-kin contact details; medication names, doses, schedules or instructions; care-plan details relating to a real service user; safeguarding details or allegation details; complaint details; incident details; any information that would identify a real service user to a third party. This list is comprehensive and matches the 4S.102B standard requirements.

**Non-replacement of senior, registered manager, medication lead, safeguarding lead and emergency services**

Present. The exact standard wording is used: *"WorkTwin does not replace your senior care worker, registered manager, medication lead, safeguarding lead or emergency services. If you are unsure what to do, call your supervisor. If there is an immediate risk to life, call 999."* This wording matches the 4S.102B requirement precisely.

**Records stay in existing care-recording systems**

Present. The exact standard wording is used: *"Real care events, incidents and observations must be recorded in Thumhara Centre's care management system, not in WorkTwin. WorkTwin is a staff guidance tool, not a record of care."* This is reinforced in Step 9 of the procedure and in Section 10 of the policy.

**AI-use note assessment:** The WorkTwin AI-use note meets the standard fully. It is one of the strongest sections of the draft. No substantive changes are required before leadership review, though the minor business impact and threshold edits noted elsewhere will strengthen the broader policy context.

## 6. Staff Usefulness Review

This section assesses whether the policy would give a trusted care worker clear, practical guidance in real refusal situations.

**What to do first**

Clearly covered. Step 1 — "Stay calm and check immediate safety" — is the first action in the procedure. It is simple, actionable and sets the right frame before the worker takes any further step. Rating: Strong.

**How to reassure**

Clearly covered. Step 3 provides calm reassurance and small choices as the third step in the sequence, after listening. Each scenario section includes specific reassurance language appropriate to that situation. The language throughout is warm, non-technical and practical. Rating: Strong.

**What choices to offer**

Clearly covered. Step 3 addresses offering choices that give the person a sense of control. Step 5 covers reasonable alternatives within the care plan. Each scenario section provides situation-specific alternatives (e.g. offering a different food, suggesting a wash rather than a shower, sitting with the person outside rather than immediately pressing them to come inside). Rating: Strong.

**When to pause and give time**

Clearly covered. Step 4 — "Allow time where safe" — explicitly addresses this, including the caveat that call time may limit how long the worker can wait, and that escalation must not be delayed indefinitely in expectation of natural resolution. Rating: Strong.

**When to escalate**

Clearly covered. Step 6 (signals to escalate), Step 7 (escalate if risk remains), Step 8 (999 for immediate risk to life) and the escalation table in Section 11 all address this from different angles. The table format in Section 11 is particularly practical as a quick reference. Rating: Strong.

**What not to do**

Clearly covered. Each scenario section (A–G) includes an explicit "What not to do" subsection. The policy statement also lists six principles including not forcing, threatening, physically moving or emotionally pressuring a service user. Rating: Strong.

**Where to record the event**

Clearly covered. Step 9 specifies what to record, using which system (Thumhara Centre's existing care-recording system), and explicitly states that WorkTwin is not a care record. Section 10 repeats and expands on this. Rating: Strong.

**When to speak to a senior or registered manager**

Clearly covered. Steps 6, 7 and 8 in the procedure address this. The escalation table in Section 11 covers nine distinct situations with specific escalation routes. Staff are also instructed at multiple points throughout the scenarios not to make decisions alone. Rating: Strong.

**Overall staff usefulness assessment:** The policy is well-structured and practically written. A trusted care worker reading it would be able to identify the correct response sequence, understand the escalation triggers and know what to record and where. The scenario sections (A–G) are a genuine strength — they translate the general procedure into specific, recognisable situations without creating a document that is too long to follow under pressure.

One area to consider in a future review: the policy does not include the estimated time frames within which escalation should occur (e.g. "if food and fluid refusal continues beyond the current visit, contact the senior before the next call"). These thresholds would strengthen the escalation guidance for less experienced workers. This is noted as a recommendation for 4S.102E or a future policy review.

## 9. Lane Decision

**Current assignment:** Lane B — cautious staff-visible support, with escalation rules.

**Assessment:** The Lane B assignment is appropriate for this policy at its current stage of development. The policy governs situations — food and fluid refusal, personal care refusal, safeguarding-adjacent risks — where general procedural guidance is useful to a care worker under pressure, but where individual service-user circumstances may change the correct course of action and a wrong answer could cause harm.

Lane B means Ask WorkTwin can provide general procedural guidance but must consistently prompt escalation and must not attempt to answer for specific service users. This matches the policy's scope and the AI-use note's prohibitions precisely.

Lane A (safe for Ask answers without consistent escalation prompts) would be too permissive given the food/fluid and safeguarding risk content of this policy. Lane C (escalation-only, no procedural answers) would be too restrictive — the policy contains genuinely useful procedural guidance that a care worker can safely apply with appropriate escalation prompts.

**Lane B is confirmed as appropriate.** This review does not challenge the assignment.

**This review does not approve the policy for live use.** The lane assignment is confirmed as correct in principle; it does not constitute approval for upload, indexing or operational use. All steps in Section 8 must be completed first.

## 10. Decision

**Pass with minor edits before leadership review.**

The draft policy is well-structured, thoroughly written and directly responsive to Shagufta's feedback. The procedure is logical and practical. The WorkTwin AI-use note meets the 4S.102B standard in full. All seven priority scenarios are covered. The policy does not contain copied QCS wording, does not include real service-user data and does not claim production readiness.

The issues identified in this review are addressable without restructuring the document. They are: a business impact rating that underestimates the risk; a policy owner field that names the organisation rather than a role; minor formatting issues in the review sheet; a missing role in the responsibilities table; and two sections (training and monitoring) that lack specificity on method and frequency.

None of these issues prevent the policy from being submitted for leadership review once the edits are applied. None require a redraft of any substantive section.

The policy must not be submitted for leadership review, uploaded, indexed or used in trusted staff practice before the edits in Section 8 are completed.
